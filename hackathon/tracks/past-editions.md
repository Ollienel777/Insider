# HackPrinceton: past editions

Copied by hand from the last two Devpost pages (Nov 2026), because Devpost
often refuses automated fetches. Use this for what judges and sponsors have
asked for before. The project galleries, which show what won, are in
`links.md`; they were not read here.

## Judging (identical in Spring 2026 and Fall 2025)

The four criteria, unweighted:

- **Creativity.** How unique is the concept? Does it address a problem you
  didn't know existed, or a common problem in a creative way or with an
  intuitive algorithm? What is the hack's "ah-ha" moment?
- **Utility.** Does it function, and how deep is the functionality? How
  likely would you be to use it frequently in your own life?
- **Charity.** Does it address a specific societal need, and how well? What
  does it contribute "in the nation's service and the service of humanity"
  (Princeton's motto)?
- **Avidity.** How excited is the team about the final state of the project?
  Shown through the presentation, the Devpost description, future plans, or
  an overall polished program.

## Submission requirements (Spring 2026 and Fall 2025 wording)

1. A link to the project's **public GitHub repository, created during the
   event dates**.
2. An **optional** demo video of at most 2 minutes, uploaded to YouTube or
   similar and linked on Devpost.
3. **In-person judging on Sunday, 9:30 AM–2 PM.** Teams present to judges in
   **at most 2 minutes**, even if a video is attached. The presentation should
   state the problem the hack solves and walk through its functionality.
4. Discord usernames on the submission, so winners can be contacted.

Judges in Spring 2026 were about 65 people, mostly working engineers and
product people from industry, plus Princeton students and sponsor staff.

- **Main tracks**: Healthcare, Sustainability, Business & Enterprise,

  Rookie; Best Hardware (Bambu Lab 3D printer); Best Game.

    reads real sources and completes real work across a company's stack.
    Criteria: 30% depth of action, 30% context quality, 40% workflow
    usefulness. Prize: a Mac mini.
  - d_model: AI research and alignment environments, mechanistic
    interpretability. $500 / $250 / $125.
  - MBZUAI: Best use of K2 Think V2, a reasoning model as the core of the
    product, not a side call.
  - Photon: agents in iMessage. $400 cash plus $400 credits; runner-up $100
    plus $100.
  - Orchids: best AI-powered app. $300 / $200.
  - Knot API: build on users' purchase data (TransactionLink, SubManager,
    AgenticShopping). Must take action on the user's behalf, not just
    analyse. $500 / $250 / $100, including "funniest use".
  - Sonar: "Beautifully Engineered Nonsense". Intentionally useless projects
    with immaculate SonarQube Cloud scans.
  - Enter Pro (8 winners), Regeneron (AI for clinical trials, $2,000 pool),
    Telora (startup track, $40K to start a company), Dedalus (containers,
    agent swarms), Justin Bojarski (Best Hardware + AI, $500 gift card, 4
    winners).
  - MLH: Gemini API, ElevenLabs, Solana, DigitalOcean, GoDaddy domain.
